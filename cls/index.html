Computational Literary Studies

The key motivation to me is to advance to theoretical modeling of complex cultural dynamics. The necessary step is to collect large-scale quantitative evidence on the history of literary communities and literary forms. I develop corpora and bibliographic data required for this work and publish them as open data.

The specific problems I currently work on: how politics affects literary content; the population dynamics of Russian children’s literature; Literary tradition, genre and stylistic innovation.

Contact kirill.maslinskii@inalco.fr

Computational literary studies 2013–

Russian children’s literature is where I apply computational methods to literary data. One line of work is corpus-based, operationalizing and measuring literary tradition and stylistic innovation; the other is bibliography-based, addressing the book market and the dynamics of the author population. Theoretical modeling of complex cultural dynamics is the larger research program that motivates both lines.

Cultural history of educational texts 2008–2010

Reading textbooks as a corpus opens a window onto the normative worldview schooling inculcates, and onto the shifts that worldview undergoes over time. Mathematical word problems are my particular interest here: miniature narratives that, taken as cultural artifacts, tell us far more than the arithmetic they contain. This is where quantitative treatment of a large body of texts first became my way of asking cultural-historical questions — the starting point of my later work in computational literary studies.

Folklore studies 2000–2014

Folklore studies is the subdiscipline that has had a formative and lasting impact on my research agenda. It is the field where each performance is seen as a link in the chain of cultural transmission, where structures and formulas in texts are interesting precisely because they are repetitive rather than unique, and where cognitive models of the social world are indispensable to explanation. These premises continue to define my interests and methodology in recent work on literary and cultural data. My research themes include historical and eschatological legends, children’s folklore, and the narrative framing of territory.
