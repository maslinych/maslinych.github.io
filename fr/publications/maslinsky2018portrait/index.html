Résumé

This article discusses a methodology for analyzing stereotypical traits in the portrayal of literary characters, using Soviet children’s literature as a case study. It presents the results of a pilot study that complements existing research on gender stereotypes in children’s literature by examining the gender-specific aspects of how child characters are depicted. We propose analyzing these stereotypical traits not merely as reflections of social norms, but as elements of the text’s poetics. We term the analysis of such phenomena in literary prose the “poetics of templates.” The study is based on a corpus of Soviet realistic prose for children and young adults spanning the 1930s to the 1980s. Through an analysis of a random sample comprising 2,486 mentions of character body parts within the corpus, we identified quantitative differences linked to character gender and uncovered a number of recurring characterization techniques involving references to body parts.
